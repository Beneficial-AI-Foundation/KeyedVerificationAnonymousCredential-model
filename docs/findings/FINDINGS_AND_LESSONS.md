# Findings and lessons from the repository record

The KVAC team, 31 October 2026.

The project formalizes in Lean 4 the keyed-verification anonymous credentials of Orrù's paper, *Revisiting Keyed-Verification Anonymous Credentials* (IACR ePrint 2024/1552), with their security proofs. This report merges the two files in which the project recorded its departures from the paper so far. The errata, [docs/presentations/rolf-status/errata.md](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/docs/presentations/rolf-status/errata.md), hold the discrepancies between the printed text and what the paper's own arguments require. The design record, [docs/DESIGN_ALTERNATIVES.md](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/docs/DESIGN_ALTERNATIVES.md), holds the decisions taken where the paper says less than a formalization needs. Both files stay in the repository with their full text, frozen, and receive no new items. This report is the record from now on, in the format of the team's reports of October 2026.

**How to add an item.** A new finding about the paper takes the next number after the highest in the file and goes at the end of the table of its category, so that numbers never change once given, one row with that number, an empty errata column, a short heading with the page, what is wrong in one sentence, the Lean object that carries the correction, its kind, its status and its origin. Its full account, the printed text, why it is wrong, the proposed correction and where the formalization records it, goes in a paragraph below the table, headed by the number. A new design decision goes into Part A6 as one bullet under the layer of the paper it concerns, or under the item of Part A it answers, with the decision, the rejected alternative and the fidelity argument in full, and the date. A lesson that the record supports goes to Part B with the items or decisions it rests on. A lesson on engineering goes to Part C and a lesson on the use of AI assistants to Part D. The status list and the "What we did" paragraphs are revised when a statement or a proof lands.

**Item 20.** Lemmas 5.4 (p. 36) and 5.5 (p. 38) write Adv^ufcmva = Adv^3-dl + …. The advantage is defined per adversary (p. 25), so the equality is a claim about every adversary. The adversary that outputs a forgery with U* = 0 never wins, since μCMZ.V rejects U = 0, so its advantage is 0 while the right-hand side is at least 1/p. A reduction yields ≤, which Theorem 5.1 (p. 35) states. The formal statements are inequalities. The writing of the Lemma 5.5 proofs document ([PR 212](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/212)) produced it, candidate 19 of [#217](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/217) since 3 October, accepted 30 October. Semar's report lists it as well, recording that the formal statements are inequalities on named reductions.

**Item 23.** Claim 5.6 (p. 39) says "The equation is non-trivial with overwhelming probability 1/p". The equation derived from the collision is non-trivial iff Σᵢ Δᵢbᵢ ≠ 0, with Δᵢ = mⱼ,ᵢ − m*ᵢ, which holds with probability 1 − 1/p. The adversary chooses Δ ≠ 0 through its queries and its forgery, while b is uniform given the adversary's view, so conditioned on any view a nonzero linear form in b vanishes with probability 1/p, and averaging over the views gives the same value (Lemma 5.6.2 of the proofs document). Claim 5.7 (p. 39) says "Assuming X₁ ≠ 0 (which happens with probability 1/p)", where Pr[X₁ ≠ 0] = 1 − 1/p. In each case the printed 1/p is the probability of the complementary event, which is the 1/p term of the claim's bound, so the bounds stand and the two sentences are backwards. No code carries the sentences. The docstrings of the gap-DL simulator and of the Lemma 5.5 statement call Σᵢ bᵢ(mⱼ,ᵢ − m*ᵢ) = 0 and x₁ = 0 the bad events, each of probability at most 1/p. The writing of [docs/CRYPTOGRAPHIC_PROOFS_MICROCMZ_LEMMA55.md](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/docs/CRYPTOGRAPHIC_PROOFS_MICROCMZ_LEMMA55.md) ([PR 212](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/212)) produced it, candidate 21 of [#217](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/217).

**What we did.** Every formal statement is an inequality on named reductions. Three bounds are stated.

- Lemma 5.4, one attribute. The paper prints Adv^3-dl + Adv^dl + 1/p. The formal bound is Adv^3-dl(B₁) + 3/p. The 3/p replaces the 1/p (item 1), and the Adv^dl term is gone (item 2).
- Lemma 5.5 and Theorem 5.1, n attributes. The paper prints Adv^3-dl + Adv^dl + 3/p. The formal bound is Adv^3-dl(B₁) + Adv^gap-dl(B_gap) + 5/p. The gap-DL term is the collision case of Claim 5.6 (item 3). The 5/p is the 3/p of Lemma 5.4 plus 1/p for the event Σᵢ Δᵢbᵢ = 0 of Claim 5.6 plus 1/p for the event x₁ = 0 of Claim 5.7. The bound is on the algebraic game with Help. Theorem 5.1 for that game is [PR 256](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/256).
- Theorem 5.11. `omufBound` and `omufBoundN1` state the printed constants. Whether they hold is decided by the proofs of Claims 5.12 to 5.14, as item 1 was for Lemma 5.4. Item 4 is recorded as an obligation on the Claims.

The 3/p is the generic Schwartz–Zippel bound for a polynomial of degree 3. The design record leaves open whether a structural argument recovers the printed 1/p, which the paper does not derive (DA *Schwartz–Zippel bound 3/p, not the paper's 1/p*).

### A6. Things the paper leaves implicit that the formalization had to decide

These are not errors of the paper, apart from items 26 and 28 and one reading of a printed φ as a typo. They are places where the paper says less than a machine needs, or where a first formalization choice was corrected. Each choice was recorded with the decision, the rejected alternative and the fidelity argument, which the design record holds in full for the entries below. The entries that answer an erratum are cited above. The rest follow, by the layer of the paper they concern.

**The proof-system layer, §3.3.**

- **Extractor and simulator exhibited existentially.** Both are exhibited in the security statements, not fields of the scheme, as in §3.3 and the standard definitions.
- **Monadic verifier.** `verify` returns `M Bool`, since the paper writes 0/1 ← ZKP.V with a sampling arrow and a Fiat–Shamir verifier recomputes its challenge through the random oracle.
- **Proveᵦ guard on the oracle side.** The Proveᵦ oracle checks (x, w) ∈ R and answers `none` on a non-witnessed query, identically in both worlds. The entry rejects a type-level guard on the query, since the check belongs to the oracle and a type restriction narrows the game's interface.
- **Relation decidability scoped to the game.** A `Decidable` instance is data, so the game that runs the guard takes it as an argument, and `relation` stays a `Prop`.
- **White-box knowledge-soundness extractor.** The extractor takes the adversary value and the run's observables, the output, the final oracle table and, for simulation extractability, the simulation log, as §3.3 has Ext take "the random coins and the code" of A. Rewinding is out of scope, the §9 instantiation being straight-line. Whether an ordered query log is needed is open.
- **Carrier `OracleComp (ZKRO H)` for setup, prove and verify.** One carrier, so `verify` can read the oracle the proof was built against. That `verify` draws no coins of its own is deferred to a lemma ([#101](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/101)), two bespoke structures that fix it in the type being rejected for their cost at every use site.
- **Structured hash domains.** `HashSpec.Dom` is an arbitrary type, a stronger model than bitstrings, with the injective encoding and the domain separation as obligations of a concrete instantiation.

**The framework, §4.**

- **Exact-attribute predicate as a family field.** `PredicateFamily` carries φ_m⃗ with its semantic law, since Figure 8's NewUsr runs issuance under it. The rest of the partial-disclosure family is [#104](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/104).
- **Correctness requires issuance to succeed before presentation runs.** The experiment asks for the event σ ≠ ⊥ ∧ b = 1, since the paper defines presentation on credentials only.
- **No obligation theorem for `ExtractablePoly`.** The theorem asserting extractability for every scheme without premises was false, and [#177](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/177) removed it. The asymptotic clause of Theorem 5.10 is [#148](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/148).
- **Anonymity game at fixed setup, negligibility over instance families.** The games take the crs, the keys, the attributes, the predicate and the initial table as parameters, as Definition 4.4 quantifies pointwise, since a game that samples setup bounds an average and an average does not bound every crs.
- **The issuer adversary runs before and after the user's request**, with random-oracle access in both runs, so that a simulator that reprograms the oracle at its first move is detectable.
- **Two adversary structures, not one pair.** A and D are separate, as the statistical and everlasting variants of Definition 4.4 constrain them separately.
- **The anonymity simulator runs in the random-oracle state monad**, so it may reprogram the table, the power §3.3 grants a simulator.
- **The Present oracle answers under the queried predicate** φ′, the printed φ being read as a typo.
- **The extractor of Definition 4.5 takes the random-oracle table** as of the call and runs in `ProbComp`, since the proof of Theorem 5.10 builds it from the proof systems' extractors, which read the trace.

**The MAC and its reductions, §5.3.**

- **Conditioned sign masks** and **conditioned η masks.** The reduction's masks are sampled conditioned on Uⱼ ≠ 0 and on H ≠ 0. The real game draws both from the nonzero elements, so the game equalities of Lemma 5.4 are false otherwise.
- **Help in the algebraic MAC game from the start.** The algebraic UF-CMVA game carries the Help arm since [PR 73](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/73), as the docstring of [KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/AlgebraicMAC.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/AlgebraicMAC.lean) and the blueprint node `agm_model` record, since the paper proves Theorem 5.1 with Help for the later proofs (p. 35). From Jin's report.

The rejected alternative is Figure 9's U ←$ 𝔾 (p. 34), under which U = 0 with probability 1/p. Eq. (1) (p. 11) draws U from the nonzero elements. ## Part B. Lessons about formalizing the paper

1. **A printed term without a reduction is unexplained, and a missing one is a gap.** The printed Adv^dl of Lemma 5.4 (item 2) and the missing gap-DL term of Theorem 5.1 (item 3) appeared when the statement had to name a reduction for each summand. Drop a term only once the bound without it has a proof, since a nonnegative summand never makes an upper bound false. A constant is derived the same way. The 3/p of item 1 and the missing Verify term of item 4 appeared when a statement had to say which event has which probability. In the code, `agm_ufcmva_le_n1_explicit` ([PR 155](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/155)) and `agm_ufcmva_le_explicit` ([PR 209](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/209)) name their reductions.
2. **An unrestricted existential reduction at a fixed finite group proves nothing.** A perfect discrete-log solver exists on a finite group, so "there exists B" without a resource bound is vacuous, and "every adversary has advantage at most ε" is false or trivial. The unforgeability statements name their reductions, while the anonymity statement still quantifies its three reductions existentially (DA *Theorem 5.11 stated before its reductions exist*). Naming a reduction fixes which algorithm the bound is about and leaves its efficiency to the asymptotic layer. The named reductions are `microCMZ3DLReduction` ([AGMReduction/Core.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/AGMReduction/Core.lean), [PR 88](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/88)), `gapDlReduction` ([PR 208](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/208)) and the three of `omufBound` ([OneMoreUnforgeability/Statements.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/OneMoreUnforgeability/Statements.lean), [PR 190](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/190)). The existential form stands in `μCMZ_anonymity` ([Anonymity.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/Anonymity.lean), [PR 228](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/228)).
3. **Ask "what if it is zero" of every sampled or received value**, in the scheme and in the reduction. It gave items 13, 14 and 15 on the scheme side, where the answer decides correctness (item 13), a security theorem (item 14) or a simulation step (item 15). On the reduction side it gave the two conditioned-mask entries, where a game equality is false until the reduction excludes the same zero the game excludes. In the code, `uniformUnits` ([PR 146](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/146)), `setup` with `uniformNonzero` ([PR 216](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/216)), `reductionMaskSample` ([SignMask.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/SignMask.lean), [PR 48](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/48)) and the η masks of [PR 229](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/229). Decide the sampling domains before the proofs. Each later change, the nonzero masks and the nonzero H, cost a lemma for every proof that read the old distribution (lesson 9 of Jin's report).
4. **One proof may analyze a smaller scheme than the theorem names.** Figure 9 and Theorem 5.3 name the token with π_is, while the proof of Theorem 5.11 analyzes the core without it (item 12). The way out is to state the smaller scheme under its own name with the printed bound, then prove the difference in a separate lemma, not yet defined ([#205](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/205)). In the code, `μCMZATCoreSyntax` ([ATVariant.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/ATVariant.lean), [PR 147](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/147)) and `μCMZATSyntax` ([ATVariantPiIs.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/ATVariantPiIs.lean), [PR 238](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/238)).
5. **Summary theorems drift from the theorems the paper proves.** Theorems 1, 5.2 and 5.3 are overview copies of §5 results, stated first and proved later (p. 35). Items 18 and 19 are copies that no revision updated, item 17 a copy that changed a term. Each summary is read on the scheme it names, which is how item 18 charges a proof the token never sends. Derive the overview statement from the theorems the paper proves, with the version history in hand. The corrected statement is `μCMZAT_anonymous_token`, [AnonymousToken.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/microcmz-theorem-5-3/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/AnonymousToken.lean) on the branch of [#184](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/184).
6. **A hypothesis on the adversary that stands in for a model restriction belongs in the adversary's type.** The hypothesis `WellBehaved` was semantic, quantified over every key, and stood in for "the adversary is algebraic". Nothing proved it was the paper's notion, and it excluded the Help oracle that a downstream proof needs. The paper proves the stronger claim one paragraph after Theorem 5.1, and the proof of Theorem 5.10 uses it. The statement to formalize comes from the proof that follows the theorem and from the proofs that use it, not from the boxed text alone. The [#235](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/issues/235) stack makes a non-algebraic adversary unrepresentable, so that the transfer lemma has no side condition. Whether that type is the paper's class is then a theorem to prove. A hypothesis that is the paper's own, such as the Sign budget q, stays a hypothesis. The gate is `AGMUFAdversary` ([AlgebraicMAC.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/AlgebraicMAC.lean), [PR 73](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/73)). Its removal is PRs [246](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/246) and [247](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/247), merged 30 October.
7. **The adversary's type is part of the theorem.** Coins ([PR 232](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/232)), the Help oracle ([PR 243](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/243)) and the encoding of the algebraic group model each changed which forgers a bound covers. Each decision came when a downstream proof needed a forger the type could not express. In the code, `OMUFAdversary` over `unifSpec` ([Preliminaries/AnonymousTokens/Security.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Preliminaries/AnonymousTokens/Security.lean), [PR 232](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/232)), `UF_CMVAHelpGame` ([UFCMVAHelp.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/UFCMVAHelp.lean), [PR 243](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/243)) and `AGMUFAdversary` ([PR 73](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/73)).
8. **The order of quantifiers decides what a simulator may see.** Placing ∃Sim before ∀A rules out a simulator that reads the adversary, which motivates a trace extractor, whose existence for the compiled proof systems is still open (DA *The anonymity simulator takes a trace extractor*). An oracle adversary that queries at any time forces two runs of the issuer (DA *The issuer adversary runs before and after the user's request*). In the code, `μCMZAnonSimulator` takes `extIs` through `risExtToTrace` ([Anonymity.lean](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/blob/main/KVAC/Schemes/MicroCMZ/Anonymity.lean), [PR 228](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/228)). ## Part C. Lessons about engineering and process

These lessons rest on the reports of Jin, Oana and Semar, not on the repository record. This part reproduces them with their authors, merging the pairs that describe one phenomenon.

1. **Two programs that compute the same distribution are not equal.** `OracleComp` is a free monad, so equality of programs is syntactic. Correctness is a statement about the output distribution or its support, never about the programs. Lesson 6 of Jin's report.
2. **A warm editor is not a build.** A lemma with the discrete log of a subtype coercion in a kernel-checked term looked proved in the editor and hung a cold `lake build` for hours, since `maxHeartbeats` does not bound the kernel. "Proved" means a cold build plus `#print axioms`. Lesson C3 of Semar's report.
3. **Instance search costs more days than mathematics.** A `SubtractionCommMonoid` diamond through the `AddCommGroup` projection of `PrimeOrderGroup` made a cold build hang until one `attribute [instance 50]` brought it to seconds. A discrete-log helper needed the `irreducible` attribute, since unfolding it in a polynomial-ring context sent typeclass search into a loop. Importing a library umbrella in place of the two modules needed turned a two-minute CI into a six-hour hang. Import narrowly, mark irreducible the definitions no proof unfolds, write an explicit instance where search loops. Lesson 7 of Jin's report, lesson C4 of Semar's report.
4. **A definitional equality that needs an unfolding is invisible to `rw` and `simp`.** Those tactics match at reducible transparency, which unfolds only definitions marked `@[reducible]` and instances, while definitional equality unfolds every definition. A lift inserted by a coercion and one written by hand elaborate with different instance terms, so `rw` and `simp` fail on goals that print identically. After the v4.30 bump `simp` stalled on binds whose type arguments differ only up to unfolding. Term-mode `bind_assoc`, `map_bind` and `bind_congr` close them, and the bridge goes into one lemma. Lesson 8 of Jin's report, lesson C5 of Semar's report.
5. **Git drops content silently.** After a squash merge every child branch of a stack conflicts, and `git rebase --onto main <old parent tip>` after each merge, one PR at a time, keeps the stack moving. A merge dropped a block of about 689 lines with no conflict, visible only as cascading unknown identifiers. The command `git rebase --update-refs` moved backup branches that sat inside the rebased range. Record the tip SHAs before every rebase and read `git range-diff` after. Lesson 5 of Jin's report, lesson C6 of Semar's report.
6. **A review is a walkthrough plus a counterexample.** Every correctness comment carries the smallest input that breaks the claim. The comments without one were the ones argued about. Lesson 10 of Jin's report.

## Part D. Lessons about the use of AI assistants

These lessons concern the machine reviewers and the coding assistants the team used. Lessons 1 to 3 come from the reports of Jin, Oana and Semar, lessons 4 and 5 from Christiano's report. None appears in another part.

1. **Machine reviewers need checking at the source.** The track ran two reviewers blind to each other. Codex was strong on counterexamples and reuse. Kimi inflated severity and once read "no sorry in the body" as "proved". The track rejected about a third of their claims. A reviewer's "this does not compile" compiled on v4.30, and a claimed `IsUniformSpec (ZKRO H)` instance did not exist. Test each claim on the toolchain, run the axiom audit after a cold build, read every diff before a commit, never let an agent push. Lesson 11 of Jin's report, lesson C8 of Semar's report.
2. **An assistant's draft is a lead, not a finding.** Drafts for PRs [213](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/213) and [216](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/216) contained an invented lemma name, a stale file count and a wrong diff count. Only the subset checked at the source went out. The rules since are agents in ask mode, every command explained before approval, every quoted line reread in the file, verified and inferred kept apart. Lesson 4 of Oana's report.
3. **Parallel reviews with sub-agents need two rules.** Each agent has its own worktree and writes its final file before handing back. The coordinator re-verifies the main claims. One day's work reviewed six PRs this way, with the coordinator restarting agents that did not resume on their own. Lesson 12 of Jin's report.
4. **The use of AI across the team has to be consistent.** Terms with no basis in the paper or in the literature, "constant audit", "collapse" and "direction" for the scalars of the reduction to n = 1, entered docstrings and blueprint nodes through one assistant, and other assistants copied them from file to file. The rule that bans coined terms in new text extends to the terms already in the code base. The team replaces them and grandfathers none. The pull request of this report carries the first replacements. Christiano's report.
5. **The code base is not a source of truth for an assistant.** The paper and the written proofs are, and the code base reflects them. When a docstring or a design entry becomes the reference an assistant reads, an error propagates. The design record's reading of Claim 5.7, that the paper's reduction owes no translation of representations, went from the design record into the module docstring and into the first draft of this report, although the paper states Lemma 5.4 in the algebraic group model (p. 36) and the written proof of Lemma 5.5 ([PR 212](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/KeyedVerificationAnonymousCredential-model/pull/212)) had already said so. Item 28 is the corrected record. Christiano's report.
